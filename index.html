A whole voxel world, grown in your browser tab.

Loamlands is a creative-mode sandbox. Type a seed and it grows forested hills, alpine lakes, snowy peaks and the caves underneath, then lets you walk, swim, fly and rebuild it block by block. No install, no account, no store page.

01 · The surface

What Loamlands is

Every world grows from a seed. Layered noise decides where oceans, beaches, plains, forests, deserts and mountains go, carves cave tunnels and ore veins below the surface, and plants trees on top. The same seed always grows the same world, so you can hand one to a friend.

You start on ground the game picks for you: a level spot with an open view of the horizon. Walk with WASD, jump, swim across a lake, or double-tap Space to fly. Break blocks, pick a new one from the hotbar and build. When you like the view, press P to keep it.

It is a prototype, not a finished game: creative mode only, no saving yet, and it wants a desktop browser with a mouse and keyboard. The rest of this page says exactly what is in, what is not, and how it is built.

03 · Features

What you can do

A small, complete creative loop rather than a long feature list. The icons below are the game's own blocks, rendered from its generated textures.

  • Seeded worlds and biomes

    Oceans, beaches, plains, forests, deserts, rocky mountains and snowy peaks, all from layered noise. Change the seed and everything changes.

  • Caves, ores and trees

    Tunnels and caverns wind under dry land, streaked with coal and iron. Trees grow in forests and plains and keep their shape across chunk borders.

  • Walk, jump, swim

    Gravity, a jump that clears one block, collision with every solid block, and water you can swim across, holding Space to reach the shore.

  • Creative flight

    Double-tap Space to fly and hold Shift to come down. Touch the ground and you are walking again. Sprint-fly is about five times walking speed.

  • Break and place

    Break instantly, with debris tinted by the block. Place from a nine-slot hotbar and hold the button to keep going. Fifteen block types, from grass to glowing lamp.

  • Streaming chunks

    The world loads in 16 × 16 chunks around you, nearest first, on a per-frame time budget. Pick a view distance from 3 to 12 chunks and the fog follows.

  • Sky, clouds and fog

    A procedural sky gradient, a drifting layer of blocky clouds, and distance fog that hides the edge of the loaded world instead of a hard cutoff.

  • Menus that keep up

    Pause, settings and controls share the game's blocky interface. Sensitivity, field of view, render scale, view distance and clouds apply immediately.

05 · Under the surface

Under the surface

For the curious: a small, conventional WebGL2 code base with no engine beyond three.js. These are the parts that matter.

  1. One draw call per chunk

    Each 16 × 16 chunk becomes at most two meshes, opaque and water. The mesher emits only faces that touch a non-opaque block and bakes directional shade and ambient occlusion into a vertex attribute, so the shader just samples a texture array and applies fog. At the default view distance that is 225 chunk meshes, about 170 draw calls after culling and 580,000 triangles.

  2. Streaming on a time budget

    Chunks are generated one ring beyond the view distance and meshed nearest-first inside a per-frame budget of 5 ms during play. Meshes that leave range are disposed; block data stays, so your edits survive the round trip.

  3. Deterministic generation

    Seeded simplex noise plus per-column hashes for trees and ores. Trees are evaluated with a three-block margin, so a canopy that crosses a chunk border is written identically on both sides.

  4. Nothing to download

    Seventeen 16 × 16 block textures are generated at start-up into a 2D texture array, and the hotbar icons are drawn from the same tiles. three.js is the only runtime dependency; the whole game is 152 KB gzipped.

  5. Tested like software

    26 unit tests cover the mesher, raycast, collision and generator. A Playwright run drives the real game in headless Chrome: breaking and placing blocks, flying, pausing, changing settings, and taking the screenshots on this page.

06 · Status

Where the project stands

Loamlands is a prototype built to prove one slice properly, not a commercial game. Here is the honest ledger.

Shipped in v0.1

  • Seeded terrain with seven biomes, caves, ores and trees
  • Walking, jumping, swimming and creative flight
  • Breaking and placing 15 block types from a hotbar
  • Menus, live settings, clouds, fog and the P screenshot key
  • Unit tests and an end-to-end browser test

Known gaps

  • Worlds do not survive a reload; edits live in memory only
  • Terrain is generated on the main thread, so flying far can cause brief hitches
  • Water is static, and lamps glow without lighting their surroundings
  • No sound, no day/night cycle, no touch controls

Next up

  • Save edited chunks per seed in IndexedDB
  • Move meshing into Web Workers
  • Biome-tinted grass and leaves, and more tree species
  • A day/night cycle with a moving sun
