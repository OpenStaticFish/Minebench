What is Blockvale?

Blockvale is a compact voxel sandbox prototype that asks a simple question: how much of a blocky sandbox can live inside one static web page? The answer, it turns out, is a lot. Everything runs locally in WebGL — terrain generation, chunk streaming, lighting, physics and UI. There is no server and no download beyond the page itself.

The world is built from seeded noise, so every reload gives you the same handcrafted feeling without any handcrafted terrain: rolling plains, dense forests, deserts, snow-capped mountains, lakes with sandy shores, and cave networks that open onto the surface. Walk, sprint and jump under real gravity — or toggle creative flight and scout the hills from the cloud layer.

Features

  • Procedural terrain

    A seeded simplex-noise heightmap builds plains, forests, deserts and mountains — biomes blend into each other instead of snapping at borders.

  • Caves that surface

    3D noise carves cave networks through the underground, complete with openings you can spot — and fall into — while exploring.

  • Chunk streaming

    The world loads in 16×64×16 chunks around you with strict per-frame budgets, so terrain appears as you move without stuttering.

  • Break & place blocks

    Raycast-targeted block edits with a nine-slot hotbar: stone, planks, logs, leaves, sand, snow, grass and more.

  • Gravity & creative flight

    Walk, sprint and jump with real collision. Double-tap Space (or press F) to toggle flight and see the world from the cloud layer.

  • Water, beaches & trees

    Lakes settle at sea level with sandy shorelines; forests scatter across grasslands with trunks, canopies and soft ambient-occlusion shading.

  • Procedural sky & clouds

    A gradient sky dome, a sun disc, and blocky drifting clouds — all generated at runtime, no image assets involved.

  • Settings that persist

    Render distance, field of view and clouds are adjustable in the pause menu and saved in your browser, so the game feels the way you left it.

  • Built-in screenshots

    Press P at any time and the browser downloads exactly what you see — the same pipeline that produced every image on this page.

Under the hood

Written in TypeScript on Three.js, built with Vite, scripted with Bun. Some choices that keep it fast:

  • Visible faces only. The mesher emits geometry solely for faces that touch air or water, so a chunk's triangle count stays proportional to its surface, not its volume.
  • Baked ambient occlusion. Soft corner shading is computed once at mesh build time and stored in vertex colors — classic voxel AO, with the quad-flip fix to avoid interpolation artifacts.
  • One atlas, few draw calls. Every block texture is generated into a single atlas at startup; each chunk renders as one solid mesh plus (optionally) one water mesh.
  • Budgeted streaming. Chunk generation and meshing are capped per frame (4 + 2), so exploration stays smooth even in software-rendered headless browsers.
  • Seeded noise stack. 2D fBm for the heightmap and biome masks, 3D noise for caves — deterministic from the seed, with no randomness you can't reproduce.
  • Tested like software. A headless Playwright harness plays the game in Chromium (software WebGL): it verifies block edits change exactly one voxel, checks gravity and collision, and captures every screenshot on this page.

Project status

Blockvale is a working prototype — not a finished commercial game. Here is an honest snapshot.

Shipped

  • Procedural world with biomes, caves, trees and water
  • Block breaking/placing with a nine-slot hotbar
  • Walking, sprinting, jumping and creative flight
  • Main menu, pause, settings with local persistence
  • Automated smoke tests and screenshot pipeline

Known limits

  • One fixed world seed per build (same terrain every session)
  • Edits last for the session only — no world saving yet
  • Static water; no flow simulation
  • No mobs, crafting, inventory or multiplayer
  • Desktop keyboard-and-mouse controls only

Next up

  • Persistent worlds and multiple seeds
  • Day/night cycle
  • More biome variety and surface decoration
  • Touch-friendly fallback controls
